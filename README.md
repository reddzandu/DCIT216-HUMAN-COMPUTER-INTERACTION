## Project Overview
The problem. Users can't trust that what they see in the app is what they'll actually get, or that the platform will help them if it isn't. This shows up as order-accuracy failures (received items not matching what was ordered), misleading coupons that don't apply as advertised, uncomfortable payment redirects to external pages, intrusive hard-to-dismiss ads, and slow, confusing navigation. The numbers back this up hard: 92% of your 13 survey respondents said page load speed shapes their first impression, and 92% have abandoned a purchase over a confusing, slow, or frustrating experience (69% definitively, 23% "maybe"). Because 54% of respondents only shop online "rarely," they have little accumulated goodwill — one bad experience is disproportionately likely to lose them for good. Your report frames this as a reliability problem, not a features problem.

Target users. Young, mobile-first, occasional shoppers with moderate (not expert) digital comfort. From your 18-person combined sample: 92% of survey respondents were 18–24, comfort with tech/apps averaged 3.5/5, and 77% shop primarily on smartphone. They're mostly occasional rather than habitual users (54% "rarely"), and they pick platforms for ease of use and convenience over price or selection. Jumia (38%) and Amazon (23%) were the most-used platforms in the survey, with Temu named specifically in three of your five interviews as a source of order-mismatch complaints.

Proposed solution. Five pillars, aimed directly at the pain points above, while keeping the features respondents already value (search, categories, reviews, secure payment, delivery visibility):

Verified order accuracy — real photos/video per listing, verified-seller badges, one-tap mismatch reporting
Simple, visible returns — return policy shown upfront on the product page, guided in-app return flow with status tracking
Transparent payments & promotions — in-app checkout (no external redirects), visible trust signals, honest coupon terms shown before applying, no fake urgency countdowns
Disciplined advertising — frequency-capped, skippable ads kept out of search/cart/checkout
Speed & reliability — compressed images, skeleton loading states, usability preserved on slow connections
